## What I Do

I work in platform engineering at Warner Bros. Discovery, helping keep the infrastructure running behind HBO Max, Discovery+, and other WBD streaming brands. 

Here's the thing about me: I came up as a software engineer. I've built systems, written plenty of Java and Python, debugged production issues at 2am. That background matters because I'm not the kind of TPM who just manages Gantt charts ; I actually understand what's happening under the hood.

These days I focus on making platform infrastructure more efficient and less painful to operate. That means a lot of cost optimization work, some automation (including experimenting with LLMs), and trying to make sure engineers spend less time fighting fires and more time building cool stuff.

## How I Got Here

I started writing code in 2014 — built POS systems and backend services for a Fortune 100 retail company. Worked on stuff that ran in 4,800+ stores. Learned a lot about systems that need to actually work when real money is on the line.

Around 2019, I started leading teams and realized I liked the orchestration part — not just building one service, but figuring out how all the pieces fit together. Got into program management, still kept coding on the side.

**Some things I've worked on:**

Built some ML-powered features that actually shipped to customers (churn prediction, revenue forecasting)
Recently: experimenting with LLMs for automating support workflows and documentation


The pattern: find expensive or painful problems, fix them with a mix of code and coordination.

## How I Think About Work

I like solving problems that are both technical and organizational. Pure coding is fun, but I get more satisfaction from figuring out why a team is drowning in support tickets and then building something to fix it.

I'm big on measurement. If we can't quantify the problem, we probably can't tell if we actually solved it. That said, not everything worth doing has a clear ROI — sometimes you just need to pay down tech debt or make life less miserable for your team.

I try to avoid buzzwords. "Leveraging synergies" makes me want to quit tech and become a carpenter. Just tell me what we're building and why it matters.
